# Content policy

Scripture is immutable within an imported edition. Exact wording, corpus, work, edition version, reference system, and UTF-8 source hash travel with every reviewed dependency. A source correction requires a versioned import and renewed editorial review. Public availability alone does not establish redistribution, adaptation, audio, or export rights. Missing evidence blocks the affected operation.

Bible, Quran, historical works, and Hadith are separate corpora. Quran references use `QUR`; Hadith cannot satisfy a Quran citation. Inventory validation uses actual edition units, including ranges and numbering differences. Historical works require their own documented subdivision/locator mapping.

Comparisons state a limited proposition. Supported relationships are shared claim, differing claim, mixed, and thematic. Mixed claims identify overlap and difference separately. Thematic similarity is not direct agreement. Keywords, classifiers, embeddings, and generated suggestions never establish religious truth. Preserve relevant Christian, Muslim, and academic interpretive distinctions with named sources.

Coverage has five independent states: unreviewed, in review, reviewed with links, reviewed with no direct match in the stated sources, and disputed. An empty list defaults to unreviewed. No-match claims require a dated human review, explicit scope, and reviewed source evidence. Disputed entries require sourced positions. Coverage denominators come from the imported corpus, not selected showcase passages.

Only an active, genuinely registered human reviewer may approve publication. The registry starts empty. No agent may invent a person, register itself as human, backdate review, or infer review from citation validity. Public reviewer records contain an ID, display name or public role, human status, and active status; private contact details stay outside shipped data.

An approval binds the claim, reference mappings, evidence IDs, exact source hashes, and all supplied explanations/translations. Each locale has its own review. Source revisions make derivative translations stale. Missing translations remain unavailable; English cannot masquerade as German, Yoruba, or Pidgin. Drafts and test fixtures are excluded from approved output.

Edition text, mood explanations, captions, context cards, and Q&A follow the same evidence and human-review gate. Treat Gen Z as a distinct Bible edition. Attribute contested historical claims and distinguish evidence from inference. Do not manufacture scholar citations or consensus. Never promise prosperity, healing, or freedom from hardship, or disparage professional care. These rules govern editorial additions; they do not alter source scripture.

All 50 Appendix A research leads remain preserved as unreviewed candidates. Their notes are investigation cautions, not approved conclusions. Candidate 48 explicitly retains the distinction between Quran 32:17 and Sahih al-Bukhari 3244. Publication requires actual permitted source text, surrounding context, a defensible proposition, and independent human judgment.
